The shipping drawer is a 24 cent line on every order

Resale ledgers love the big lines: the buy price, the marketplace fee, the label. The drawer next to the desk never gets a line of its own. Poly mailers, tape, labels, and dunnage are consumables, and consumables are per-order costs with the same standing as a fee schedule. They eat zero seconds of labor by themselves, so every cent of the drawer lands on the dollar side of the hourly math.

Price one shipped order. A 10x13 poly mailer runs 11 cents. A 4x6 thermal label runs 4 cents. Tape comes off a $2.40 roll that is 55 yards, or 1,980 inches, so a 12 inch strip costs about 1.5 cents and three strips close a mailer for 4 cents. Two sheets of kraft dunnage add 5 cents. The drawer total is 24 cents an order. At 40 shipments a month that is $9.60 monthly and $115.20 a year, and against six minutes of pick, pack, and label time it works out to $2.40 an hour off the quoted rate. A listing that says $25 an hour is paying $22.60 once the drawer gets a line.

The one upgrade worth pricing is the padded mailer, and it is a wash where it looks strongest. Rigid goods in an 11 cent poly arrive damaged about 1.8 percent of the time; at $22 a claim, refund plus return label, that is 40 cents of expected loss and 51 cents all in. A 42 cent padded mailer cuts damage to half a percent, 11 cents of expected loss, 53 cents all in. Two cents is a coin flip. On soft goods the poly wins outright: damage near zero keeps the poly order at 13 cents all in, and padded is a 40 cent premium for protection the product never needed.

The drawer has a gadget trap too. A $130 thermal printer saves 6 cents a label against 10 cent inkjet sheets, which pays back after 2,167 labels, about four and a half years at 480 labels a year. It is a volume tool, not a starter tool. The free box is the most expensive thing in the room: cutting old tape and re-folding a used box adds three minutes of labor, $1.25 at a $25 target rate, to save a mailer and two dunnage sheets worth 16 cents.
